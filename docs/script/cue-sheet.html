Video calling is almost a hundred years old.

▸1927 — AT&T shows the first public video call.

▸1964 — Bell Labs' Picturephone launches, and flops.

▸The nineties put cameras on home PCs.

▸The two-thousands built life-size telepresence rooms.

Through all of it, one thing never changed: what video conferencing is.

What kept changing was the infrastructure underneath.

That infrastructure is what we revisit.

8Session Lifecyclesession-lifecycle · 4:15 · ~37s

One session, end to end.

▸×3A node registers — stake and capability. A caller creates a room, and an escrow that funds it.

▸×2Assignment: the cp-daemons score candidates, and a quorum — more than two-thirds — ratifies the pairing on-chain.

▸×2Setup issues ICE, DTLS, and TURN credentials.

▸×2Then media runs — the SFU forwards, nodes heartbeat.

▸×4Close ends the session, and settlement pays the relay — or records a slash obligation.

Evidence flows on-chain. Packets don't.

9Dual-Signed SessionProofsession-proof · 4:52 · ~51s

This is the accountability core.

▸×2One validator holds two wallets — an identity wallet, and a session wallet publicly mapped to it.

▸×3Both sign the same nine-field measurement message: room, relay, packets, bytes, peers, duration, latency, loss, jitter.

▸×2Both public keys and both Ed25519 signatures go into the transaction.

▸×2The chain verifies them, stores the measurement — and discards the signatures.

Two things to be exact about.

This is dual-signing, not consensus — validators agree only later, at settlement.

And the probe leans on relay-reported counters; it does not prove this relay served this room.

That binding gap is ours, and it's in the limitations.

Say "dual-signed", NEVER "consensus-signed". State the binding gap unprompted (W-03).
